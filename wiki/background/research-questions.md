# Research Question

## Current object

Study the 林爽文事件 as a problem of wartime communication and command.

## Main question

How did early Qing reports, imperial responses, and fragmented command arrangements turn an apparently suppressible disturbance into a war requiring exceptional intervention by 福康安?

## Supporting questions

- When and through which documents did the court recognize the scale of the crisis?
- How were 黃仕簡 and 任承恩 expected to cooperate?
- Where do reports show delay, filtering, uncertainty, or competing command channels?
- When did imperial confidence in the initial response break down?
- How did upward reports and downward orders frame responsibility and military urgency?

## Method boundary

Official documents are evidence of communication and bureaucratic framing, not transparent descriptions of battlefield reality. Preserve source wording, date semantics, and uncertainty.

## FYP working emphasis

The FYP materials sharpen the project around **information delay and central
control**. The review should compare the dates and contents of frontline
reports, Fujian mediation, imperial responses, and later official replies to
ask how the court acted when its information was already out of date.

The working comparison uses different information positions rather than only
biographies: 柴大紀 as a Taiwan-frontline reporter, 李侍堯 as a Fujian-side
information and logistics intermediary, 常青 as the earlier commander whose
reports became a problem for the court, 福康安 as the later commander sent to
rebuild frontline control, and 阿桂 as a central adviser. These roles are
analytical guides; claims about each person must still be grounded in the
canonical documents.

The corresponding teaching explanation should make the method visible:
extract source-bound records, preserve send/receive/issue semantics, connect
documents and events, calculate elapsed time where the dates support it, and
then review the AI proposals before presenting a cautious interpretation. The
secondary and FYP notes provide context for this framing, not substitutes for
the primary-source evidence.
