# Competition context

This folder contains the local copy of the **2026 PolyU AI X Digital
Humanities Awards** brief:

[`2026 PolyU AI X Digital Humanities Awards_AI_Classics(v.1).docx`](<2026 PolyU AI X Digital Humanities Awards_AI_Classics(v.1).docx>)

The document is labelled a bilingual public draft. It contains reviewer
comments, incomplete contact fields, provisional timetable details, and some
formatting/weighting inconsistencies. Use this README to orient project work,
but confirm the official competition webpage, registration link, dates, judging
weights, and enquiry contact before publishing them.

## What the draft says

- **Theme:** “AI & Classics”, understood broadly as classical texts, canonical
  thought, premodern history and culture, rare-book images, translation, and
  related knowledge communication.
- **Participants:** full-time undergraduate students in eligible PolyU Faculty
  of Humanities programmes; entries may be individual or teams of 2–4, with one
  official entry per student/team.
- **Possible formats:** research-oriented work, tool-oriented work,
  education-oriented work, and exhibition-oriented work. The examples include
  generative AI, NLP/NER, knowledge graphs, GIS, data visualisation, AI agents,
  interactive teaching materials, AR/VR, and multimodal storytelling.
- **First-round materials:** application/project abstract, a project
  introduction video of no more than five minutes, a PDF/PPT presentation,
  optional prototype/code/dataset/model/GitHub or testing link, an AI-use
  statement, and source or licensing acknowledgements.
- **After shortlisting:** revised presentation materials; a demonstrable
  version, recorded demonstration, or testing link for tool, website, system,
  teaching, exhibition, or AI-agent entries; and a 1,000–1,500-word explanatory
  note for research- or teaching-oriented entries. The draft says a testing
  link should remain accessible to judges for at least three months.
- **Indicative timetable:** the draft places the webpage/call and training in
  2026, gives 30 September 2026 as the registration deadline and 31 December
  2026 as the first-round deadline, and places shortlisting/final presentation
  in January 2027. Several month fields are incomplete or inconsistently
  formatted in the source, so these dates require official confirmation.
- **Judging directions:** problem definition and relevance to Classics or the
  humanities, feasibility/functionality and technical realisation, innovation,
  scholarly quality and research rigour, social impact/sustainability and
  responsible AI, and presentation skills. The draft's visible weighting table
  is not yet reliable enough to reproduce as final rules.
- **Integrity and responsibility:** disclose AI tools, prompting approach,
  generated-content scope, human revision and verification; do not fabricate
  sources or citations; and address copyright, privacy, research ethics,
  accuracy, context, bias, and AI limitations.

## Relevance to this repository

The review zhu/yu system can be presented as a combined research, tool, and
teaching example. Its student-facing companion website should demonstrate the
following reproducible chain:

`source extraction -> data organisation and annotation -> relationship and timing analysis -> human review -> digital presentation`

The historical explanation can draw on the material in
[`../2nd Material & FYP/README.md`](<../2nd Material & FYP/README.md>): the
林爽文事件 and its battles, Qing governance and military organisation,
奏摺／硃批／上諭 and information transmission, information-delay calculations,
new political history, and map/GIS context. The website should make clear that
secondary notes provide background, while the canonical source dataset and
human-reviewed outputs provide the evidence used by the system.
